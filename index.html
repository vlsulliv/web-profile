01 — About

Fieldwork, first principles, and a stubborn need to understand how things fail.

I'm a petroleum engineer who spends as much time in reservoir simulators as in code editors. My work sits at the intersection of subsurface engineering and the tools we use to make sense of it — production data, well logs, and the models that turn both into decisions worth staking money on.

Outside of fieldwork, I build things: small internal tools, home automation projects, and the occasional over-engineered spreadsheet. I like problems that reward patience — whether that's a well that takes months to reveal its real behavior, or a piece of code that only starts to make sense on the third rewrite.

Lately I've been trying to close the gap between what an engineer knows in their head and what actually gets written down — better notes, better tools, fewer things re-learned the hard way.

02 — Engineering

Core disciplines

The layers I work across most often, roughly ordered by where they sit between reservoir and wellhead.

Reservoir Engineering

Modeling how fluids move underground and translating that behavior into a recovery strategy the rest of the team can act on.

Drilling & Completions

Turning a subsurface plan into a well that actually delivers — and adjusting when the rock disagrees with the plan.

Production Optimization

Finding the marginal gains between the reservoir and the wellhead: artificial lift, flow assurance, and surface constraints.

Simulation & Modeling

Building and stress-testing the models everything else depends on — and knowing when to distrust them.

Data & Automation

Writing the scripts and internal tools that make repeat analysis fast, checkable, and less dependent on memory.
